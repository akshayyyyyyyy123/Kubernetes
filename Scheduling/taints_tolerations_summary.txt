(*) Taints & Tolerations

- Taints and tolerations are used to control which Pods are allowed to run on specific nodes.
- A taint is applied to a node to repel Pods that don't have a matching toleration.
- A toleration is defined in the Pod specification and tells the scheduler that the Pod is allowed to be scheduled on a node with that matching taint.
- Taints and tolerations are commonly used to dedicate nodes for specific workloads and prevent unwanted Pods from being scheduled there.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(*) End-to-end flow

- Suppose we have:
    Node-3
  label: role=database
  taint: role=database:NoSchedule

- The taint means: 🚫 "Don't schedule Pods here unless they tolerate me."
- Now consider a Pod.


Case 1 — No toleration

       Pod
        ↓
  Node-3 has taint
        ↓
  Pod doesn't tolerate it
        ↓
  ❌ Cannot schedule


Case 2 — Matching toleration

      Pod
        ↓
  Has matching toleration
        ↓
  Taint allows it
        ↓
  ✅ Node-3 becomes eligible

- But here's the important interview point: "A toleration does not tell the scheduler to choose that node. It only makes the Pod eligible for that tainted node."
- If you want the Pod to specifically choose the database node, combine the toleration with:
    nodeSelector:
      role: database
or Node Affinity.

- So remember:

                  NodeSelector / Affinity
                          ↓
                  "WHERE do I want to run?"
                  
                  Toleration
                          ↓
                  "AM I ALLOWED to run there?"
                  
                  Taint
                          ↓
                  "WHICH Pods should stay away?"

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

🔥 One-liner for an interview

"Taints are node-side rules that repel Pods, while tolerations are Pod-side permissions that allow Pods to run on tainted nodes. 
Tolerations don't attract Pods to a node; they only make them eligible."


(*) Three Taint Effects
    (i) NoSchedule
    - Don't schedule new Pods unless they tolerate the taint.

    (ii) PreferNoSchedule
      - Try not to schedule Pods here. It's a soft restriction.

    (iii) NoExecute
      - This is stronger.
      - Don't schedule new Pods here AND evict existing Pods that don't tolerate the taint.


| Effect             | Meaning                                                        |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `NoSchedule`       | Don't schedule new non-tolerating Pods                         |
| `PreferNoSchedule` | Try to avoid scheduling non-tolerating Pods                    |
| `NoExecute`        | Don't schedule them and evict existing non-tolerating Pods     |
